Why do people enjoy being scared?

The short answer

Up to a point. In a 2020 field study, 110 visitors to a haunted house in Denmark wore heart-rate monitors and rated their fear and enjoyment. Enjoyment peaked at a middle level of fear: too little was dull, too much was unpleasant.

Fear is an animal’s alarm. It means: get away from here. Humans pay to walk into dark houses where actors jump out at them.

Researchers from Aarhus University fitted 110 visitors, aged 12 to 57, with heart-rate monitors and filmed them at the scariest moments. Plotted against fear, enjoyment made an upside-down U: highest in the middle, lower at both ends.

The researchers describe this as a sweet spot of arousal that feels just right, and treat recreational fear as a kind of play.

What we checked

110 haunted-house visitors (ages 12 to 57) enjoyed it most at a middle level of fear.

Sources and notes

  • Andersen, Schjoedt, Price, Rosas, Scrivner & Clasen (2020), "Playing With Fear: A Field Study in Recreational Horror", Psychological Science 31(12): 1497–1510. doi.org

Questions people ask

Is there a perfect amount of scary?

On average, in this study, yes: enjoyment was highest at a middle level of fear and fell when visitors were barely scared or very scared.

How was fear measured?

Visitors rated their own fear and enjoyment, wore heart-rate monitors, and were filmed at the scariest points of the house.
