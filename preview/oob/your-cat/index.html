Do cats know their names?

The short answer

Yes. In a 2019 study of 78 cats in Japan, cats reacted more to their own names than to similar-sounding words or to other cats’ names, even when a stranger was speaking. Coming when called is a separate matter.

Humans keep a small predator in the house. It hunts, it has claws, and it ignores them. They clean up after it every day. In return, it purrs. Humans consider this a fair deal.

The part about ignoring is supported by research. Scientists in Tokyo played cats a list of four ordinary words that sounded like their names, then the name itself. The cats lost interest during the list, then perked up at their own name: ears, head or tail moved.

The study measured recognition, not obedience. A cat that knows its name and stays on the sofa is behaving exactly as the data describes.

Sources

  1. Saito, Shinozuka, Ito & Hasegawa (2019), "Domestic cats (Felis catus) discriminate their names from other words", Scientific Reports 9: 5394. Link
  2. Scientific American, "Cats Recognize Their Own Names, Even If They Choose to Ignore Them". Link

Questions people ask

Why does my cat ignore me when I call its name?

Recognising a name and acting on it are different things. The study found cats notice their names; it did not find that they come when called.

Does a cat understand that the name means itself?

That is not known. The researchers suggest cats may simply link the sound of the name with food, petting or play.
