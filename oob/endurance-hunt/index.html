Can a human run down an antelope?

The short answer

Sometimes, in great heat. Hunters in the central Kalahari were recorded chasing kudu for about 2 to 5 hours, over 25 to 35 km, at 40 to 42°C. The animal overheats and has to stop; the human, cooled by sweat, keeps going.

A kudu can outrun any human over a short distance. The hunters do not try to. They keep it moving through the hottest part of the day and track it whenever it slips out of sight.

The animal overheats and has to stop. Humans sweat across the whole body, so they can keep going in heat that stops a faster animal. In one hunt Louis Liebenberg measured, the hunters covered about 35 km in 3 hours 35 minutes.

This rests on a small number of recorded hunts. Scholars still debate how common persistence hunting was in the past, and how much it shaped human evolution.

Sources and notes

Questions people ask

Could a person today run down an animal?

Only in narrow conditions: great heat, open country, a hunter who can track, and hours of steady running. Liebenberg recorded it among a few hunters in the central Kalahari.

Is this why humans can run long distances?

It is one proposed explanation. It is debated, and the evidence from living hunters rests on a small number of recorded hunts.
